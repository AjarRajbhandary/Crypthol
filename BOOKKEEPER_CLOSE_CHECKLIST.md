# Bookkeeper Review Checklist — Closing the Books

A close checklist for a multi-entity, multi-channel e-commerce group. Written for a
year-end close ahead of the tax return; the same list works as a monthly or quarterly
close with the year-end-only items skipped.

## How to use this

Work top to bottom. Nothing is final until every balance sheet account has a schedule
behind it that a third party could follow. The P&L takes care of itself when the balance
sheet is right — most e-commerce bookkeeping errors show up as a wrong balance, not a
wrong expense.

Assumed shape of the business: several related entities with shared people and overlapping
spend; sales across multiple marketplaces plus a direct storefront; advance funding against
marketplace receivables; payroll plus overseas contractors; inventory split across
fulfillment-center, third-party-logistics, and in-transit locations.

---

## 1. Define "final" before starting

- [ ] Which entities are in scope, and is anything reported on a combined basis?
- [ ] Cash basis or accrual basis for the books, and does the tax return use the same basis?
- [ ] Prior-year closing balances match the last **filed** return's balance sheet, line for
      line. If they don't, find out why before touching the current year.
- [ ] Every adjusting journal entry the accountant gave for the prior year is actually
      booked in the accounting system, not just reflected in the return.
- [ ] Set a target lock date. After sign-off, a closing-date password goes on the file.

## 2. Cash, cards, and lenders

- [ ] Every bank account, credit card, and payment platform reconciled through period end
      with a **zero** unreconciled difference.
- [ ] Uncleared checks and deposits older than 90 days investigated and cleared or voided.
- [ ] Any account used for both business and personal is split explicitly — the personal
      share goes to owner draw, not to an expense.
- [ ] Accounts opened or closed mid-year are all on the chart of accounts. An account nobody
      reconciles is where errors hide.

## 3. Marketplace settlements — the highest-risk area

Deposits are not revenue. Every settlement has to be decomposed, or gross sales, fees, and
refunds are all wrong at once.

- [ ] Each settlement split into gross product sales, refunds and returns, fulfillment fees,
      referral or commission fees, storage, advertising, and reserve movement. Never book the
      net deposit as sales.
- [ ] Any marketplace reserve held at period end recorded as a receivable. It's your money,
      just not released yet.
- [ ] Direct storefront: gross orders reconciled to processor payouts; processing fees as
      expense; gift cards and store credit as a liability, not revenue.
- [ ] Retail-media and advertising invoices recorded in the month the ads ran, not the month
      they were paid.
- [ ] Channel-integration and software vendor fees recorded, including anything unpaid at
      period end sitting in accounts payable rather than missing entirely.
- [ ] Channel mapping (class or location tracking) consistent across all twelve months.
      Changing the mapping mid-year makes trend analysis useless.
- [ ] Chargebacks, claims, and reimbursements recorded — reimbursements for lost or damaged
      units are income, and the matching inventory has to come off.
- [ ] Form 1099-K from each platform reconciled to book revenue; the difference (fees,
      refunds) is explainable in writing.

## 4. Advance funding and merchant financing

- [ ] Advances recorded as a **liability**, never netted against marketplace revenue.
- [ ] Fees or discounts on advances booked as financing or interest expense, not as a
      marketplace fee.
- [ ] Liability balance at period end ties exactly to the funder's statement.
- [ ] Card spend drawn against a funding line is recorded as expense on the spend date and
      reduces the available balance — not double-counted again on repayment.

## 5. Inventory and COGS

The single biggest driver of a wrong tax number for a physical-products business.

- [ ] Physical count or system quantities at period end, captured **separately** for each
      location: marketplace fulfillment centers, each third-party warehouse, own warehouse,
      and in transit.
- [ ] Goods shipped but not yet received are still yours if the shipping terms say so —
      record inventory in transit.
- [ ] Inventory valued at **landed cost**: unit cost plus inbound freight, duties and tariffs,
      inspection, and prep. If those are hitting the P&L as period expenses, inventory is
      understated.
- [ ] Costing method (FIFO or weighted average) applied consistently all year and consistent
      with the prior year.
- [ ] Inventory roll-forward ties: beginning + purchases − COGS ± adjustments = ending. If it
      doesn't tie, COGS is wrong.
- [ ] Gross margin reviewed **by month by channel**. A large swing in one month almost always
      means a misposted purchase or a missed inventory adjustment.
- [ ] Write-offs, removals, disposals, and unsellable stock documented with quantities and
      reasons — not a plug entry.
- [ ] Deposits paid to suppliers for goods not yet produced are a **prepaid asset**, not COGS.
- [ ] Confirm with the tax preparer how inventory is treated for tax purposes; small-taxpayer
      methods can differ from the book treatment, and that should be a decision rather than an
      accident.

## 6. Payables, receivables, and cutoff

- [ ] Supplier invoices dated in the period but received later are accrued — check the
      following weeks' mail and inbox.
- [ ] Period-end advertising spend accrued even when billed in the next period.
- [ ] Credit card charges dated in the period but posted after it land in the right year.
- [ ] Prepaid expenses (annual software, insurance, deposits) sit in a schedule and amortize;
      the closing balance is supported line by line.
- [ ] Accrued bonuses documented, with deductibility timing confirmed with the tax preparer.
      For an S corporation, accrued compensation to a more-than-2% shareholder is not
      deductible until paid, and other accrued bonuses generally must be paid within two and a
      half months of year end.
- [ ] Receivables aging reviewed; anything uncollectible written off with a reason.

## 7. Payroll and contractors

- [ ] W-2 totals and employer taxes tie to payroll expense in the general ledger, quarter by
      quarter.
- [ ] Payroll-provider tax reconciliation adjustments and refunds are posted, not floating in
      a clearing account.
- [ ] S-corporation officer compensation recorded through payroll at a defensible amount —
      a frequent audit trigger.
- [ ] Shareholder health insurance, HSA, and other fringe benefits included on the W-2 where
      required.
- [ ] Contractor payments of $600 or more identified, W-9s on file, 1099-NECs issued. Payments
      made by card or third-party processor are excluded — the processor reports those on
      Form 1099-K.
- [ ] Overseas contractors have Form W-8BEN on file and are **not** issued 1099s.
- [ ] Payroll clearing and liability accounts net to zero, or to a balance that ties to an
      actual amount owed.

## 8. Intercompany and owner activity

- [ ] Due-to and due-from balances between entities agree in both directions and net to zero
      across the group.
- [ ] Any expense one entity paid on another's behalf is either pushed to the right entity or
      recorded as an intercompany balance — not buried in the payer's P&L.
- [ ] Owner money movements classified correctly: payroll, distribution, loan, or capital
      contribution. These are not interchangeable.
- [ ] Personal expenses run through business accounts reclassified to owner draw unless there
      is a documented business purpose.
- [ ] Shareholder basis and accumulated adjustments account schedules updated for an
      S corporation — distributions in excess of basis are taxable, and the return can't be
      right without this.
- [ ] Related-party loans have a written note and stated interest.

## 9. Sales tax and filings

- [ ] Nexus reviewed for the year, including new states triggered by warehouse locations or
      volume thresholds.
- [ ] Marketplace-facilitator-collected tax separated from tax collected directly on your own
      storefront. The second kind is yours to remit.
- [ ] Sales tax liability account clears to amounts actually remitted; a growing unexplained
      balance means missed filings.
- [ ] State annual reports, franchise taxes, and registered agent fees current for each entity.

## 10. Balance sheet scrub

Every account, no exceptions:

- [ ] Fixed assets: additions listed, depreciation schedule updated, disposals removed.
- [ ] Loans: balance ties to the lender's statement; payments split between principal and
      interest per the amortization schedule.
- [ ] Uncategorized income and expense, "ask my accountant," and suspense or clearing accounts
      are **zero**.
- [ ] No negative inventory, negative payables, or negative receivables balances.
- [ ] Opening balance equity is zero.
- [ ] Retained earnings rolls forward correctly and matches the prior filed return.
- [ ] Duplicate transactions scanned for — a common artifact of bank feeds, manual entry, and
      integration imports all running at once.

## 11. Analytical review before sign-off

- [ ] Twelve-month P&L reviewed side by side for outliers. A month three times its neighbors
      is an error until proven otherwise.
- [ ] Gross margin by month and by channel is stable and plausible.
- [ ] Revenue by channel compared against each platform's own annual report.
- [ ] Any account whose full-year total looks wrong for the business traced back to source
      documents.

## 12. Package to hand the tax preparer

- [ ] Trial balance at period end
- [ ] P&L and balance sheet — full year, and by month
- [ ] General ledger detail
- [ ] Bank and credit card reconciliation reports with matching statements
- [ ] Inventory valuation schedule by location
- [ ] Fixed asset and depreciation schedule
- [ ] Loan statements and amortization schedules, including any funding-provider year-end
      statement
- [ ] Payroll: W-3, W-2s, quarterly returns, state filings, annual payroll reports
- [ ] 1099s issued, with the W-9 and W-8BEN file
- [ ] Marketplace annual summaries and 1099-Ks per channel
- [ ] Intercompany reconciliation across entities
- [ ] A written list of open questions, assumptions, and anything that had to be estimated

## 13. Sign-off and lockdown

- [ ] Bookkeeper signs off in writing that the above is complete, and lists what remains open.
- [ ] Closing date set with a password so the period can't drift.
- [ ] Backup or export of the file saved outside the accounting system.
- [ ] After the return is filed, the preparer's adjusting entries are booked back into the
      books so next year's opening balances match the return.

---

## Deadlines to confirm

For calendar-year filers, extended S corporation and partnership returns are due September 15;
extended individual and C corporation returns are due October 15. Confirm with the tax preparer
which entity falls where, and whether anything is already late — if it is, that changes the
order of work.

## Questions to settle before starting

1. Which entities are in scope?
2. Cash or accrual, and does that match the return?
3. Is marketplace settlement decomposition automated, or done by hand? Manual decomposition
   across several channels is the most likely place for a close to go wrong.
4. Was a physical inventory count taken at period end, and did any warehouse migration move
   stock across the period boundary?
